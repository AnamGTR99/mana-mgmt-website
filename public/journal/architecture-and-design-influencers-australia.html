Architecture and design influencers in Australia: who they reach and how brands work with them

Architecture and design influencers in Australia are a small group of creators whose audience is students, young architects, designers and the people who buy the software, furniture and tools they use. The useful ones are judged on reach to non-followers and on what a post made people do, not on follower count.

Who counts as an architecture or design influencer?

Three kinds of accounts show up when brands search this niche. Practising architects and studios who post their work; students and young graduates who film the life around studying and working in design; and visual creators who make motion, 3D and concept films for brands and treat architecture as a subject. Most lists online mix all three with interior stylists and home-decor accounts, which is why they are hard to book from.

For a brand the split matters. A studio account sells its own services and rarely takes paid posts. A student or young-professional creator reaches the people who are choosing software, buying their first desk setup and following tutorials. A motion or 3D creator is hired to make the content itself rather than to post it to a following. Decide which of the three you need before you look at a single profile.

What does the audience actually look like?

The lists you find online give follower counts and little else. Follower counts tell you almost nothing in this niche, because design content travels on Instagram's recommendation feed far more than on the follow graph. The numbers that matter are monthly views, the share of viewers who do not follow the account, saves, and where the viewers are.

One Melbourne example from the MANA roster, Kevin Chiang, makes architecture, design and student-life reels. His Instagram insights for a 30-day window in June 2026 showed 928K+ monthly views, 40K average views per reel and 92% of reach coming from people who do not follow him, with 359K unique viewers and 34.7K saves in the month. 88% of that audience is aged 18 to 34, split evenly between men and women, with Melbourne as the single biggest city and India, Australia and the United States as the top three markets.

Those are the figures to ask any creator in this niche for. A screenshot of insights for the last 30 or 90 days, showing views, non-follower reach and audience age, is a normal request and a creator with a real audience will send it the same day.

  • Monthly views and average views per reel, from Instagram insights, with the date range shown
  • Share of reach from non-followers (above 70% means the content travels on recommendations)
  • Saves and shares, which matter more than likes for tutorial and tools content
  • Audience age and top cities, so you know whether you are reaching students, professionals or both
  • Where the audience is: many Australian design creators reach India, the US and Southeast Asia as much as Australia

What do brands book architecture and design creators for?

Design software and plug-ins, 3D and rendering tools, hardware for creative work, desk and studio setups, drawing and modelling supplies, furniture and lighting aimed at young professionals, and education products such as courses and apps. The audience is young, technical and in a buying phase, which is why software brands in particular come back.

The content that works is a tutorial, a tools list or a day-in-the-life that shows the product doing something real. One organic reel by Kevin showing a modelling plug-in in use reached 490K+ views and drove 100+ paid sign-ups for the software, with no paid boost and no whitelisting. A reel on five tools an architecture student should know reached 161K views with 11K saves and produced 25 sign-ups for a mobile app. Neither mentioned a price or ran as an ad.

The pattern in both: the product was shown inside the creator's normal format, the brand supplied the facts it needed in writing, and the creator wrote the script. Brands that script the reel themselves get the reach of a brand account, not of the creator.

How much do architecture influencers in Australia charge?

Rates in this niche are set per deliverable: an organic reel, a carousel, a story set, a reel with a partnership ad, raw UGC footage or an edited UGC reel. Three things move the price: usage (whether and for how long you can run the content in your own channels or ads), whitelisting (running it as an ad from the creator's handle), and exclusivity (whether the creator stays away from competitors, and for how long). Software and B2B briefs often carry a small premium because the content takes longer to get right.

Architecture, 3D and urban planning: the adjacent creators

Brands that search this niche usually have a second need nearby. Some want a motion or 3D creator to make a launch film rather than a posting creator; on the MANA roster that is Hugo Zbor, a Melbourne creative director who makes green-screen concept films, motion and CGI, with 40 or more projects shipped. Others want the student side of the audience, where Preston Young, a University of Melbourne urban planning student, makes day-in-the-life and student-life reels to a male-led audience aged 18 to 34.

If the brief is a product for architects and designers, start with the architecture creator. If it is a visual launch, start with the motion creator. If it is a campus or student product, start with the student creator. One point of contact at MANA can scope all three in one media kit.
